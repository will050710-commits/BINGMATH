Given:
- Triangle \(ABC\) where \(AB = 6\), \(AC = 4\), and \(BC = 5\).
- Internal angle bisector of \(\angle A\) intersects \(BC\) at point \(D\).
- External angle bisector of \(A\) intersects extended side \(BC\) at \(E\).

We need to find the length of segments \(CD\) and \(CE\).


1. **Calculate the length of AD using Stewart's theorem**:

   According to Stewart's theorem for triangle \(ABC\) with cevian \(AD\):
   
   \[
   b^2m + c^2n - amc = d(b^2 + cn)
   \]
   
   Here,
   - \(a = BC = 5\),
   - \(b = CA = 4\),
   - \[c = AB = 6,\]
   - \(d = AD\),
   - Let \(BD = m\) and \(DC = n\). Since \(B\) divides into two parts in ratio as per given lengths:
     \[
     BD : DC = AB : AC = 3 : 2 
     \]

    Therefore, we have:
    
    \[
    m = \frac{3}{5} \times 5 = 3; \quad n = \frac{2}{5} \cdot 5 = 2
    \]

2. **Apply Stewart’s formula**: 

   Substituting values from above equation,

   \[
   4^2(3) + 6^2(2) - 5 \cdot 6 \cdot 2 = d(4^2 + 6 \cdot {2})
   \]

   Simplifying further,

   \[
      96 + 72 - 60 = d(16+12)
   \]

   Thus

   \[
       108 = d \cdot 28
   \]

   Solving for \(d\):

   \[
        d = \frac{108}{28}
   \]

   Hence,

   \[
        AD = \frac{54}{14} = \frac{4}{7}\sqrt{13}.
   \]


3. **Lengths of \(CD\) and EC**:

   Using the Angle Bisector Length Formula:

   For internal angle bisector \(AD\),

   \[
   AE = \frac{\left| AB^{2}-AC^{2} \right| }{AB+AC}= \frac{|6^{2}-4^{2}|}{6+4}=\frac{20}{10}=2
   \]

For external angle bisector \(AE\)

Using the property that the external angle bisector extends beyond vertex \(C\):

\[EC = AE + AC = 2 + 4 = 6.\]

Thus, the lengths required are:

\[
CD = 2 \text{ units}, \quad CE = 6 \text{ units}.
\]
